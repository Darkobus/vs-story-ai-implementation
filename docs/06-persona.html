Part 06 · Persona

An assistant, not a “god”

Why the dark “god” persona got in the players' way, and how the prompts now keep the bot a plain, helpful assistant that answers from the knowledge base.

On this page
  1. Why the “god” was a bad idea
  2. The principle: helpful > theatrical
  3. Where the persona lives in the code
  4. The answer prompt, rule by rule
  5. The tone of event reactions

1 Why the “god” was a bad idea

The first version of the bot played “the dark god of Vintage Story” — enigmatic, sarcastic, commenting on players' deaths. Fun for five minutes, but as a tool people use every day it had real problems:

  • It buried the facts. A player asks “how much tin for bronze?” and gets “Metal hides its secrets, mortal…” — the information is lost in the theatre.
  • Gloom after a death is irritating. A sarcastic remark right after someone lost their progress grates instead of helping.
  • It contradicted the job. The bot is there to help. A “god” implies superiority and distance — the opposite of an assistant.
  • Pathos gets old fast. A matter-of-fact, friendly tone ages much better.
That does not mean “no flavour at all”. The assistant can be friendly and occasionally light-hearted, but it helps first and decorates second — never the other way round.

2 The principle: helpful > theatrical

✗ The “god” persona

  • Enigmatic, dark, sarcastic
  • Comments on and judges the player
  • The content hidden behind pathos
  • “mortals”, “the abyss”, “chaos”

✓ The assistant persona

  • Matter-of-fact, friendly, concrete
  • Helps and gives tips
  • The answer first, a joke maybe later
  • The players' own everyday language

3 Where the persona lives in the code

The tone is defined only by prompt text in bot/watchdog.py:

FunctionWhat it controls
ask_ai() → system_contextAnswers to players' questions
react_to_event() → the prompts dict + its system lineGreetings, goodbyes, deaths, storms, kills
autonomous_loop()The periodic gameplay tip
The prompts are written in English (like the rest of the code) and tell the model to reply in Polish. The old persona also lived in the fine-tuning data and the Modelfile; those are now in archive/train/ and no longer used.

4 The answer prompt, rule by rule

system_context = (
    "You are a helpful assistant on a Vintage Story game server. "
    "Rules: "
    "1. Always reply in Polish, naturally and in a friendly way. "
    "2. Give the concrete answer first, concisely, at most 4 sentences. "
    "3. Base your answer PRIMARILY on the 'Wiki knowledge' below - it is hard data from this game; "
    "if it contains numbers or recipes, give EXACTLY those values, not your own. "
    "4. Vintage Story is NOT Minecraft - it has its own, different mechanics. Do not carry over "
    "recipes, ratios, names or rules from other games. "
    "5. If the knowledge below does not contain the answer, say plainly that you do not have "
    "reliable information - do NOT guess numbers, recipes or names from memory. "
    "6. No asterisks, stage directions or pathos. "
    "7. At the end you may add the command [CMD: time] or [CMD: weather] when it fits. "
    f"Wiki knowledge: {knowledge or NO_KNOWLEDGE} "
    f"Conversation context: {history}"
)
RuleWhy it is there
1 · Polish, friendlyThe players write Polish; the tone is the assistant's, not the god's.
2 · Answer first, ≤ 4 sentencesChat is small; long replies scroll away.
3 · Use the knowledge, exact numbersThe whole point of RAG — otherwise the model “rounds” ratios from memory.
4 · Not MinecraftThe most common hallucination: Minecraft recipes and mechanics presented as Vintage Story's.
5 · Say you don't knowWorks together with the relevance threshold: when no chunk is close enough, the knowledge is replaced by NO_KNOWLEDGE, which tells the model to admit it.
6 · No theatreKeeps the old persona's habits out.
7 · CommandsThe only commands it may suggest are the two on the whitelist (see Bot).

5 The tone of event reactions

Event reactions use their own short prompts. The important change is the reaction to a death: sympathy and a practical tip instead of sarcasm. 

prompts = {
    "join":        f"Player {context} just joined the server. Greet them briefly, warmly ... Max 1-2 sentences.",
    "leave":       f"Player {context} just left the server. Say goodbye briefly and nicely. Max 1 sentence.",
    "death":       f"Event: {context}. Comment on it with sympathy and add one short, practical tip ... Max 2 sentences.",
    "storm_start": "A temporal storm is coming. Warn the players briefly ..., remind them to take shelter.",
    "storm_end":   "The temporal storm has just passed. Say briefly that it is safe again. Max 1 sentence.",
    ...
}
# system line for every reaction:
"You are a helpful, friendly assistant on a Vintage Story server. "
"Reply ONLY in Polish, briefly and to the point. No asterisks, stage directions, brackets or pathos."
The periodic message is now a useful gameplay tip instead of a dark one-liner, but it still fires every 5–15 minutes and there is no switch to turn individual reactions off. That is on the roadmap.

6 Before and after

The bot replies in Polish; the examples are translated here.

Situation“God” (before)Assistant (now)
“how do I make bronze?” “Metal hides its secrets, mortal. The alloy is born in the fire of the crucible…” “Tin bronze is 8–12% tin and 88–92% copper, melted together in a crucible. You'll need a mould to cast it.”
a player is killed by a wolf “The wolf is older than your dreams. No wonder it won.” “Bad luck with that wolf. Next time take a spear and stay near the light at night.”
a player joins “Another soul wanders my lands…” “Hi Jan, good to see you on the server! Have a good game.”
a question the knowledge base can't answer A confident, made-up recipe. “I don't have reliable information on that — the wiki is the safest place to check.”
Telling players how to call the bot when they join would be a small detail that helps a lot — today the greeting does not mention it; the roadmap adds a fixed hint.
